principles:
  - title: A module boundary is explicit or it isn't real
    body: Packages only hold up when the dependencies between them are declared and verifiable. Without that, "modular" is just folder organization.
  - title: Stability is work, not luck
    body: Crashes have root causes, and root causes show up in Crashlytics clusters — not in guesses. Treating shipping as an engineering event changed the product more than any refactor.
  - title: Shipping can't depend on someone remembering
    body: While releasing requires a person following a checklist, delivery frequency is capped by that person's calendar. Automating signing and submission is an architectural decision.
  - title: Documentation needs a gate in CI
    body: Docs nobody is required to update will rot. Tying architecture documentation to a pipeline check is what keeps it true.

I've been building mobile apps since 2017, almost always in Flutter. In that time
I've done a bit of everything: a cooperative's app I maintained on my own, greenhouse
automation controlled from a phone, investment journeys in a regulated environment
and, these days, a live sports scoreboard with Live Activities written in Swift. I
also broke an app into thirteen packages, replaced manual delivery with an automated
process, and went from senior developer to mobile engineering manager in five months.
Alongside that I run a consultancy, where I deliver the whole product: app, API,
database, infrastructure.

I started in 2017 at Fundação Uniselva, on my own. Recoopsol, a cooperative's
e-commerce platform, was mine from the Flutter app down to the Django server with
PostgreSQL. There was nobody to review my code. That's terrible for quality and
excellent for learning consequences. In 2021 I added Osiris Agtech, where the problem
was more physical: hydroponics automation, with irrigation, nutrient solution and
sensors controlled from a phone. That's where I used MobX and Clean Architecture for
the first time in a real app, and also worked on what sat behind it, modeling data
with GraphQL, Prisma and PostgreSQL.

In 2022 I joined Genial Investimentos, first through TIVIT and then through Squad
Premium. It was two and a half years on a high-traffic investment app with very little
room for error. I worked on credit cards, bank slips, insurance products and
CMS-driven content, using Cubit for state and EventBus to get features that didn't
know about each other talking. I learned there how to build animations and transitions
that survive production, and how to design a component thinking about whoever reuses
it. It's also where it became clear I wanted to decide architecture instead of just
implementing the one already in place.

I joined R10 Score in October 2024 as a senior developer. A month later I was leading
the team, five months after that I was accountable for mobile engineering. What got me
there was taking on the native side. In Swift, with ActivityKit and WidgetKit, I wrote
the chronometer that follows the match phase, the countdown before kickoff, the league
table shifting position live and the penalty shootout updating on the lock screen kick
by kick, with the Flutter app on the other side talking over Platform Channels. On
Android my role is integration: Gradle, FCM, Billing, platform channels.

Then came the things that never show up on screen. Thirteen packages with declared
dependency boundaries. Notifications isolated into their own package. In-app purchases
with StoreKit. gRPC and WebSocket for data that has to arrive now. Crashlytics driving
actual fixes. And releases that stopped being a manual ritual.

In the consultancy, over the same period: a loss-control system with a Flutter mobile
app and a React web app, a multi-tenant platform in NestJS with Prisma, an odds
pipeline with EV calculation, Terraform and CI.

I write here about what survived production and what I'd do differently. Half of what
I know came from people who bothered to write it down.
