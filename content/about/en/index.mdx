I've been building mobile apps since 2017, almost always in Flutter, and what I've
done in that time ranges from a cooperative's app I maintained alone to a live
sports scoreboard with Live Activities written in Swift. Along the way: greenhouse
automation controlled from a phone, investment journeys in a regulated environment,
an app broken into thirteen modular packages, manual delivery turned into an
automated process, and a move from senior developer to mobile engineering manager in
five months. In parallel, I ship complete products through my own consultancy — app,
API, database and infrastructure.

I started in 2017 at Fundação Uniselva, and I started alone: Recoopsol, a
cooperative's e-commerce platform, was mine from the Flutter app down to the Django
server with PostgreSQL. There was nobody to review my code — terrible for quality,
excellent for learning consequences. In 2021 I added Osiris Agtech, where the problem
was more physical: hydroponics automation, with irrigation, nutrient solution and
sensors controlled from a phone. That's where I used MobX and Clean Architecture for
the first time in a real app, and worked on what sat behind it — data modeling with
GraphQL, Prisma and PostgreSQL. Five years learning that an app is the visible tip of
a system.

In 2022 I joined Genial Investimentos, first through TIVIT and then through Squad
Premium — two and a half years on a high-traffic investment app with very little room
for error. I worked on credit cards, bank slips, insurance products and CMS-driven
content, with Cubit for state and EventBus for communication between features that
didn't know about each other. That's where I learned to build animations and
transitions that survive production, to design components thinking about whoever
reuses them, and to operate under financial regulation with design, server and QA in
the same cycle. It's also where it became clear that I wanted to decide architecture,
not just implement the one already in place.

I joined R10 Score in October 2024 as a senior developer; a month later I was leading
the team, and five months after that I was accountable for mobile engineering. What
got me there was taking on the native side: in Swift, with ActivityKit and WidgetKit,
I wrote the chronometer that follows the match phase, the countdown before kickoff,
the league table shifting position live, and the penalty shootout updating on the lock
screen kick by kick — with the Flutter app on the other side, talking over Platform
Channels. On Android my role is integration: Gradle, FCM, Billing, platform channels.
Then came the things that never show up on screen and hold up everything else —
thirteen packages with declared dependency boundaries, notifications isolated into
their own package, in-app purchases with StoreKit, gRPC and WebSocket for data that
has to arrive now, Crashlytics driving actual fixes, and releases that stopped being a
manual ritual. In the same period, in my consultancy: a loss-control system with a
Flutter mobile app and a React web app, a multi-tenant platform in NestJS with Prisma,
an odds pipeline with EV calculation, Terraform and CI. I write here about what
survived production and what I'd do differently — half of what I know came from people
who bothered to write it down.
